\large Evidence from the BVIV Perpetual on Volatility Forecasts, Funding Costs and 15-Minute VWAP Timing}
\author{Gregor Albiez}
\date{October 2026}

\begin{document}
\maketitle
\vspace{-1.2em}

\begin{abstract}
\noindent Bitcoin implied volatility is tradable: a perpetual future on the Volmex \BVIV{} index has traded on Bitfinex since April 2024. We use 15-minute data from \LiveStart{} to \LiveEnd{}: Binance BTC prices, the official Volmex index, and the full Bitfinex record of marks, premia and \LiveFundN{} funding settlements. With them we ask how BTC volatility should be forecast when a 30-day implied index is observable, and whether a long-\BVIV{} position, possibly timed by a volume-weighted average price (VWAP) signal on 15-minute bars, hedges BTC. HAR augmented with implied variance is the best forecast at one day, significantly better than HAR, and at seven days; the bias-corrected index is best at 30 days. Out of sample, with rules frozen on simulated training data, a minimum-variance \BVIV{} hedge reduced the expected shortfall (ES) of a long-BTC book by \LiveEsAlways\% (95\% block-bootstrap CI \LiveEsAlwaysLo--\LiveEsAlwaysHi\%), almost entirely through a handful of crash days. The costs are real. Funding settled at its $\pm$0.25\% cap in \LiveFundCapShare\% of 8-hour periods and cost a permanently long contract \LiveFundAnnualPts{} vol points a year, the perpetual traded a median \$\LiveMedianUsd{} a day, and executing one hour late cut the ES reduction to \LiveDelayHour\%. A VWAP ratchet adds about two points of ES reduction, not significantly, and placebos with the same triggers at shifted dates do as well: the gain is hedge size, not timing. A calibrated simulation with an arbitrage-free perpetual and \NTest{} test paths reproduces the forecast ranking and the timing result. \BVIV{} perpetuals work as a crash hedge, but funding and capacity, not signal design, decide what that hedge is worth.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}\label{sec:intro}

A holder of bitcoin, in spot or through a perpetual swap, faces two kinds of risk: an ordinary random walk, and sudden crashes in which liquidity disappears and implied volatility spikes. Futures hedges remove the first and, with it, the position itself; crash protection has required options. The Volmex \BVIV{} index measures 30-day BTC implied volatility in a model-free way \citep{volmex2024iv}, and it has traded as a USDT-margined perpetual on Bitfinex since April 2024 \citep{coindesk2024bitfinex}, on gTrade since June 2025 \citep{coindesk2025gtrade} and on Hyperliquid since September 2026 \citep{coindesk2026hyperliquid}. CME announced Bitcoin Volatility futures in May 2026 \citep{cme2026bvi}.

Whether long volatility hedges long BTC depends on the spot-vol correlation, which in BTC is weak and unstable. Positive return shocks have historically raised volatility more than negative ones \citep{bouri2017return,baur2018asymmetric}, an asymmetry that has faded \citep{takaishi2021time}; the BTC--DVOL correlation flipped positive in early 2023 \citep{coindesk2023dvol}, while \BVIV{} trended down through the 2024--25 rally \citep{coindesk2025rally}. Equity investors learned that long-volatility hedges look good \emph{ex ante} but are eroded by carry and costs \citep{whaley2013trading,alexander2016diversification,israelov2019pathetic}, except in genuine crises \citep{szado2009vix}. This raises two questions: what does such a hedge cost on a real crypto venue, and can it be \emph{timed} by a signal every intraday trader watches, price relative to its VWAP?

\paragraph{Contributions.} (i) We provide, to our knowledge, the first evidence on hedging BTC with a tradable implied-volatility perpetual, including a reconstruction of its funding rule and of its realised costs. (ii) We compare seven volatility forecasts on the official \BVIV{} index. (iii) We test VWAP timing with rules frozen on simulated data, single-path block-bootstrap inference and a \emph{timing placebo} that separates timing information from hedge size. (iv) A calibrated simulation with an arbitrage-free perpetual serves as a controlled experiment. A pipeline regenerates every number from public data.

\section{The BVIV index and its perpetual}\label{sec:background}

\paragraph{Index.} \BVIV{} is a 30-day constant-maturity implied-volatility index that Volmex describes as model-free \citep{volmex2024iv}; secondary sources list Deribit and OKX options as inputs, and Volmex serves it at 15-minute resolution. Deribit's DVOL applies a VIX-style variance-swap calculation \citep{demeterfi1999guide} to Deribit options alone \citep{deribit2021dvol}. Implied variance exceeds subsequent realised variance on average: \citet{almeida2024risk} estimate about 0.14 a year in variance units for 2017--22, consistent with \citet{alexander2021bitcoin} and the equity evidence of \citet{carr2009variance}.

\paragraph{The Bitfinex perpetual.} One contract pays one dollar (USDt) per index point; the mark price is the index converted at USD/USDt, and over our sample it deviates from the official Volmex index by less than \LiveMarkGap\% on 95\% of 15-minute bars. Funding settles every eight hours. The status feed exposes the accrued premium of the perpetual over its mark and two clamp parameters, 0.05\% and 0.25\%, and the settled rates follow $\mathrm{sign}(a)\min\{\max(|a|-0.05\%,0),0.25\%\}$ of the accrued premium $a$ (Section~\ref{sec:data}). The cap, 0.75\% of notional a day, binds often: in \LiveFundCapShare\% of the \LiveFundN{} settlements from April 2024 to October 2026, and long holders paid on \LiveFundPosShare\%. A permanently long contract paid \LiveFundAnnualPts{} vol points a year on average (\LiveFundAnnualPct\% of notional), but the burden swung from \LiveFundPtsA{} points in 2024 and \LiveFundPtsB{} in 2025 to \LiveFundPtsC{} in 2026, when longs \emph{received} funding (Figure~\ref{fig:livemarket}). The market is tiny. The perpetual traded on \LiveDaysTraded{} of \LivePerpDays{} days, with a median daily volume of \$\LiveMedianUsd{} (mean \$\LiveMeanUsd) and a median open interest of \LiveMedianOi{} contracts; its order-book mid sat a median \LivePremiumMedian\% above the mark.

\section{Data and volatility forecasts}\label{sec:data}

\paragraph{Data.} BTC is the Binance BTCUSDT spot market on 15-minute bars (\LiveDays{} days); quote volume over base volume gives each bar's exact VWAP. \BVIV{} is the official Volmex series from its public 15-minute history. The perpetual's marks, order-book mids, open interest and accrued funding come from about 1.3 million Bitfinex status snapshots (one a minute), from which we reconstruct every funding settlement; the reconstructed rule matches the settled rates with a correlation of 0.985. BTC-perpetual funding for the perpetual-book variant is Bitfinex's own. Table~\ref{tab:livefacts} summarises the sample. Volatility averaged \LiveRv\% against a mean index of \LiveIv{} (range \LiveIvMin--\LiveIvMax); implied exceeded subsequent realised volatility on \LiveIvAbove\% of days. The daily spot-vol correlation was \LiveCorrDaily{} overall but moved from \LiveCorrA{} in 2023 through \LiveCorrB{} and \LiveCorrC{} to \LiveCorrD{} in 2026: the market became VIX-like. Fifteen-minute index changes are autocorrelated (\LiveAc), a trace of the index's smoothing that matters for execution (Section~\ref{sec:live}).

\paragraph{Forecasts.} From 15-minute log returns we compute daily realised variance \citep{andersen2003modeling}; fifteen minutes is coarser than the 5-minute rule of thumb \citep{liu2015does} but matches the hedge's frequency. Each model forecasts the average daily variance over the next $h\in\{1,7,30\}$ days from information at day $t$'s close: a random walk, RiskMetrics EWMA \citep{jpmorgan1996riskmetrics}, GARCH(1,1) with Student-$t$ errors re-estimated monthly, the heterogeneous autoregressive (HAR) model of \citet{corsi2009simple} with a 7-day week, the index itself, raw ($\IV_t^2/365$, ``IV-raw'') and bias-corrected (``IV''), and HAR augmented with implied variance:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:har}
\ln\overline{\RV}_{t+1:t+h}=\beta_0+\beta_d\ln\RV_t+\beta_w\ln\overline{\RV}_{t-6:t}+\beta_m\ln\overline{\RV}_{t-29:t}+\beta_{iv}\ln(\IV_t^2/365)+\varepsilon_{t+h}.
\end{equation}
HAR and IV are the special cases without the implied or the realised terms. Regressions run in logs, so forecasts $\exp(\hat y+s^2/2)$ are positive, and are re-estimated daily on an expanding window of targets already observed. We evaluate the \LiveForecastN{} one-day forecasts from 2024 on with QLIKE, which is robust to proxy noise \citep{patton2011volatility}, the Mincer--Zarnowitz $R^2$ and Diebold--Mariano (DM) tests against HAR with Newey--West variance \citep{mincer1969evaluation,diebold1995comparing}.

At one day HAR-IV is best (QLIKE ratio \LiveQlOne) and significantly better than HAR (DM \LiveDmOne), while the index alone is worse (\LiveQlIvOne) and GARCH on daily returns lags (\LiveQlGarchOne), as \citet{bergsli2022forecasting} found for BTC. At longer horizons the differences are not significant. HAR-IV stays ahead at seven days (\LiveQlSeven; DM \LiveDmSeven), with the bias-corrected index close behind (\LiveQlIvSeven); at 30 days the index is best (\LiveQlThirty; DM \LiveDmThirty), followed by HAR-IV (\LiveQlHarThirty), and correcting it for the variance risk premium helps (raw IV \LiveQlRawThirty). The calibrated simulation has the same winner at every horizon (Section~\ref{sec:sim}). The 30-day HAR-IV forecast feeds the hedge sizing below.

\section{Hedging framework}\label{sec:hedge}

\paragraph{P\&L.} The book is long $q$ BTC at price $S_t$ and long $H_t\ge0$ \BVIV{}-perpetual contracts with mark $F_t$. A position chosen at the close of 15-minute bar $t$ earns over bar $t{+}1$
\begin{equation}\label{eq:pnl}
\Pi_{t+1}=q\,(S_{t+1}-S_t)-\mathbb{1}_{\text{perp}}\,q\,S_t f_t\,\Delta t+H_t\,(F_{t+1}-F_t)-H_t\,\psi_t-(H_{t+1}-H_t)(P_{t+1}-F_{t+1})-c\,|H_{t+1}-H_t|\,F_{t+1},
\end{equation}
where $\Delta t$ is the bar length in years, $f_t$ the BTC perpetual's annualised funding rate, $\psi_t$ the \BVIV{} funding per contract, booked on the position held at each settlement, $P_t$ the perpetual's order-book mid, so buying above the mark is a cost, and $c$ a proportional cost of \Fee{} bp fee plus \Slip{} bp slippage. On live data $\psi_t$ is the settled funding. A perpetual with funding intensity $k$ is priced at the expected index sampled at a random time \citep{he2022perpetual,ackerer2025perpetual}; in the simulation we split its funding into an arbitrage-free drift transfer $\E_t[I_{t+1}]-I_t$ and a carry premium.

\paragraph{Hedge ratios and signal.} The minimum-variance hedge is $H_t^\ast=-qS_t\beta_t$, with $\beta_t=\mathrm{Cov}_t(r,\Delta F)/\mathrm{Var}_t(\Delta F)$ estimated on 15-minute changes by an EWMA (half-life 14 days) \citep{johnson1960theory,ederington1979hedging}; we impose $H\ge0$. A downside ratio $\beta^-_t=\E_t[r\,\Delta F\mid r<0]/\E_t[\Delta F^2\mid r<0]$ on falling bars, shrunk towards $\beta_t$, gives the crash hedge $H^-_t$. The breakdown statistic compares the price with its rolling 24-hour VWAP,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:z}
z_t=\ln\!\big(S_t/\mathrm{VWAP}^{24h}_t\big)\big/\big(\hat\sigma_t\sqrt{1/3}\big),
\end{equation}
because for Brownian prices the gap between the last price and its average over a window $w$ has standard deviation $\sigma\sqrt{w/3}$. Here $\hat\sigma_t$ is a causal EWMA (half-life one day) of 15-minute squared returns, de-seasonalised by a trailing hour-of-week profile \citep{andersen1997intraday,hansen2024periodicity}.

\paragraph{Rules.} All rules are long-only, capped at 50\% of BTC notional, and trade only when the target leaves a 25\% band around the position \citep{whalley1997asymptotic}. \emph{Always-on} holds $\lambda H^\ast_t$. The \emph{VWAP-switch} holds $H^-_t$ while a gate is on, switching on at $z_t<z_{in}$ and off at $z_t>z_{out}$. The \emph{VWAP-ratchet} holds a core $\varphi H^\ast_t$ plus an overlay $o_t$ that jumps to 1 on a breakdown and decays with half-life $\tau$:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:ratchet}
o_t=\begin{cases}1 & z_t<z_{in}\\ 2^{-1/(96\tau)}\,o_{t-1} & \text{otherwise,}\end{cases}\qquad
H_t=\varphi H_t^\ast+o_t\max\!\big(H^-_t\,m_t-\varphi H_t^\ast,\,0\big),
\end{equation}
where $m_t\in[0.5,1.5]$ optionally scales the overlay by forecast ``cheapness'', the log ratio of the 30-day HAR-IV forecast to implied variance.

\paragraph{Protocol.} Rule parameters are \emph{frozen}: they were selected on \NTrain{} simulated training paths (Section~\ref{sec:sim}), out of 72 ratchets and six switches, by median ES reduction net of costs. The selected ratchet keeps the full core ($\varphi=\RatchetFloor$), triggers at $z<\RatchetZ$, decays with a half-life of \RatchetHl{} days and uses forecast sizing; the selected switch enters at $z<\SwitchZ$ and exits at $z>\SwitchExit$. The live sample is therefore pure out-of-sample evidence, which avoids the snooping trap of choosing among many rules on the evaluation data \citep{white2000reality,hansen2005spa,bailey2014pseudo}. Signals warm up on 2023 data, and hedging is evaluated over the \LiveEvalDays{} days on which the perpetual traded, \LiveEvalStart{} to \LiveEvalEnd. On a single path we report 95\% intervals from a paired stationary block bootstrap over days (mean block ten days, 2{,}000 draws). The timing placebo shifts the ratchet's trigger series circularly by every multiple of a week, which keeps the triggers' number, clustering and hour-of-week profile but breaks their link to the market.

\paragraph{The hedge works, in crashes.} The minimum-variance hedge reduced ES by \LiveEsAlways\% (block-bootstrap CI \LiveEsAlwaysLo--\LiveEsAlwaysHi\%) and variance by \LiveVarAlways\% (\LiveVarAlwaysLo--\LiveVarAlwaysHi\%), and cut the maximum drawdown from \LiveMddUnhedged\% to \LiveMddAlways\% with an average notional of \LiveNotionalAlways\% of the BTC position (Table~\ref{tab:livehedge}); it was held on \LiveTimeOnAlways\% of bars and switched off whenever the estimated spot-vol covariance turned positive. It is a crash hedge in the literal sense (Figure~\ref{fig:livehedge}). Net of funding, its P\&L summed to \LiveHedgeTotal\% of BTC notional: the ten best days contributed \LiveTopTen\% and all other days \LiveExTopTen\%. On the ten worst BTC days, which cost the unhedged book \LiveWorstBtc\%, the hedge earned back \LiveWorstHedge\%. The best day, \LiveBestDay, returned \LiveBestDayPct\% of BTC notional, when \BVIV{} rose from 57 to 96 within hours as BTC fell by 14\%. Figure~\ref{fig:liveepisode} shows that day at 15-minute resolution. BTC broke through its VWAP band at 14:00 UTC, after \BVIV{} had jumped from 61 to 74 in two hours; it reached 96 by 21:45. All rules entered the day with 560--610 contracts. As the index's 15-minute moves exploded, the EWMA hedge ratio fell and the rules sold into the spike, which kept the hedge's dollar risk roughly constant; the ratchet's overlay kept its position 13--20\% above the always-on hedge's. Funding cost the hedge \LiveFundAlways\% of BTC notional a year on average: it paid \LiveFundHedgeA\% a year in 2024 and \LiveFundHedgeB\% in 2025 and received \LiveFundHedgeC\% in 2026.

\section{The controlled experiment}\label{sec:sim}

A single path cannot separate rules reliably, so we repeat the study in a simulated market calibrated to the stylised facts (Table~\ref{tab:livefacts}, last column). Log-variance is the sum of a slow and a fast Ornstein--Uhlenbeck factor; the fast factor tracks a three-state hidden regime (calm, stress, euphoria) and receives EGARCH-type shocks \citep{nelson1991conditional} whose signed loading on the return shock is $-0.30$\slash$-0.60$\slash$+0.25$ by regime, so volatility rises in sell-offs and, in euphoria, in rallies. Jumps cluster in stress; volatility and volume follow intraday and weekend seasonality. The index is the square root of the model's exact expectation of 30-day average variance (a Feynman--Kac product over regime paths), scaled by a variance premium and perturbed by persistent sentiment. The perpetual's funding is the exact one-bar drift transfer plus a carry of \HedgeCarry{} vol points a year, so it offers no free lunch from mean reversion; unit tests check both expectations against brute-force simulation. Each path has \SimDays{} days; rules are selected on \NTrain{} training paths and evaluated on \NTest{} test paths.

\paragraph{Findings.} The forecast ranking is the same as on live data: HAR-IV is best at one day (median QLIKE ratio \QlHarIvOne) and seven days (\QlHarIvSeven), the bias-corrected index at 30 days (\QlIvThirty). The hedging results have the live pattern at a smaller scale (Table~\ref{tab:simhedge}), because the simulated spot-vol correlation (\CorrDaily) is weaker than in 2025--26. The always-on hedge reduces ES by \EsRedAlways\%; the switch trails it by \DEsSwitchAbs{} pp. The ratchet beats it by \DEsRatchet{} pp (95\% CI [\DEsRatchetLo, \DEsRatchetHi]) but its placebo by only \DEsPlacebo{} pp, and a 1.5$\times$ static hedge reaches the same ES reduction (\EsRedAlwaysOnehalf\%). Across the grid, timing adds \PgridFloorZero{} pp without a core and \PgridFloorOne{} pp with the full core, the same gradient as on live data. The ratchet's edge over the always-on hedge (Table~\ref{tab:robust}) survives doubled or halved trading costs and a carry of 0 to 24 vol points a year (\RobCostMin{} to \RobCostMax{} pp; \RobFundingCarryTwentyfour{} pp at the live average of 24). It shrinks to \RobInverseLeverage{} pp under inverse leverage and to \RobCrashesClusterInStress{} pp when crashes cluster in stress.

\section{Limitations and conclusion}\label{sec:conclusion}

The live sample is one path of \LiveEvalDays{} days, whose tail is dominated by a few crashes, and the hedge's gains depend on them; the block bootstrap quantifies but cannot remove this. Results assume fills at the 15-minute close at the observed mid plus costs, which the book supports only for a few bitcoins, and the delay results show how much depends on prompt execution. Oracle risk on builder-deployed venues \citep{hyperliquid2026funding} and the 15-minute boundary's algorithmic order flow \citep{kim2026quarter} argue for working orders inside the bar, for example by VWAP slicing \citep{berkowitz1988total,konishi2002optimal,bialkowski2008improving}. The simulation's spot-vol dynamics are calibrated, not estimated.

Four lessons follow. (1)~Forecast BTC volatility with HAR augmented with implied variance up to a week, and with the bias-corrected implied index at a month. (2)~A minimum-variance \BVIV{} hedge cut tail risk substantially in 2024--26, because the market turned VIX-like and crashed with spiking implied volatility; it should be held, not switched. (3)~Its price is funding, which on Bitfinex is capped, volatile and usually paid by longs, and its limit is capacity: the perpetual traded a median \$\LiveMedianUsd{} a day, and its book held the hedge of a few bitcoins. (4)~A 15-minute VWAP breakdown is real but secondary information: it beats random timing without a core hedge, but with one a ratchet's gain is size, not timing. Any intraday timing rule should beat a placebo with the same trigger statistics before it earns its complexity.
